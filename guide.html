Welcome to MediStock Cloud

MediStock Cloud runs every shop of your business from one app. It works with or without internet: sell, buy and count stock offline, and everything uploads by itself when the phone is online. Tap a heading below to open it.

★ How to add a New Drug (start here)

This is the part new users find hardest, so take it slowly. Go to Inventory → + Add Drug. Under each box the app prints dim help text; this page explains the same things with examples.

The big idea: a medicine is packed in layers, like a box of sweets. You tell the app the layers once, from the biggest pack you buy down to the smallest piece you sell. After that, selling, buying, counting and alerts all use your own names (Packet, Strip, Tablet…) and the app does the arithmetic.

Step 1 — Name
Type the name with the strength as on the pack: Paracetamol 500mg. This is what you will search when selling. The generic name, class, dosage form and supplier are optional helpers.

Step 2 — Packaging (the important part)

  • Stock unit = the BIGGEST unit you buy and store. Examples: Packet, Box, Bottle, Carton.
  • Base unit = the SMALLEST piece you ever sell. Examples: Tablet, Capsule, ml, Sachet, or Bottle if you never open it.
  • Middle level (Intermediate unit) = only if there is a pack between the two, like a Strip inside a packet. Tick the box and name it. If there is no middle level, untick the box.

Then give the numbers. They answer: how many of the smaller thing fit in ONE of the bigger thing?

  • With a middle level: Strips per Packet and Tablets per Strip.
  • Without a middle level: Base units per stock unit (e.g. 100 capsules in one box).

Below the boxes the app shows a sentence such as 1 Packet = 100 Tablets (10 Strips × 10 Tablets). Read it. If it matches the real pack, you filled it correctly.

Worked examples

  • Paracetamol 500mg — 1 packet has 10 strips, each strip has 10 tablets.
    Stock unit: Packet · Base unit: Tablet · middle box ticked · Intermediate unit: Strip · Strips per Packet: 10 · Tablets per Strip: 10.
  • Cough syrup — sold by the bottle only.
    Stock unit: Bottle · Base unit: Bottle · middle box unticked · Base units per stock unit: 1.
  • Amoxicillin capsules — box of 100, sold loose.
    Stock unit: Box · Base unit: Capsule · middle box unticked · Base units per stock unit: 100.
  • Diclofenac injection — box of 10 ampoules.
    Stock unit: Box · Base unit: Ampoule · unticked · 10.
  • Skin cream — one tube is one item.
    Stock unit: Tube · Base unit: Tube · middle box unticked · Base units per stock unit: 1.

Step 3 — Prices (UGX)

  • Wholesale cost = what YOU pay the supplier for ONE stock unit (one packet). It is used to work out your profit.
  • Retail prices = what the CUSTOMER pays: for a whole packet, for one strip, for one tablet. Fill the levels you really sell; leave the others empty. At the till you can still change the price of a sale.

Step 4 — Reorder level and target stock (in packets)
Reorder level is the number at which the app warns you (and sends an alert). Target stock is how many you want after restocking; the "What to Buy" screen uses it to tell you how many to order.

Step 5 — Opening stock
Count what is on the shelf right now, from big to small: complete packets, opened strips, loose tablets. Example: 3 full packets + 2 strips + 4 tablets → boxes 3, 2 and 4. The line below shows the total.

Opening stock is only for a drug's first stock. When more arrives later, record it in Purchases, never here, otherwise it is counted twice.

Step 6 — Save Drug.

After saving

  • The quantity is locked. To correct it use Stock Taking (it records a reason and keeps the history).
  • You can still correct the packaging (names and how many are in each) if you made a mistake. The quantity you hold does not change; only the way it is counted.
  • Edit the name, prices, reorder level any time. You return to the same place in the list.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the number of packets where tablets per strip goes. Always read the sentence the app shows under the boxes.
  • Adding more stock in both "Opening stock" and "Purchases".
  • Typing the retail price of a packet in the tablet box (a tablet price should be small).
  • Ticking the middle box for a drug that has no strips; untick it and use Base units per stock unit.

1. Getting started

Owner: open the app, choose Owner → Create business account. Enter your name, business phone, business name, first shop name, shop location and a short shop code (for example MAIN), then your email and password.

Your name is printed on receipts as the cashier. The shop location and phone are printed on receipts too.

Forgot your password? On the Owner log-in screen tap Forgot password? under the password box (type your email first). A reset link is emailed to you. Check Spam too.

Operator (shop worker): choose Operator and enter the Shop Code, your Operator ID and your 6-digit PIN. Tap Show to see the PIN you typed.

The first time a new phone opens the app it needs internet to download your shop data. After that it opens fast, even offline.
2. The Dashboard

The Dashboard shows sales, profit, expenses, number of sales, stock alerts and your cash. Pick a period (today, week, month…) and, as owner, pick one shop or All shops.

Real cash available is the cash you should have in the till. Tap the eye icon on the card to hide the amount (an eye with a line through it) and tap again to show it. It is the same figure as on the Cash Position screen and the choice is remembered on the phone.

3. Selling
  1. Open Sell and type part of the drug name in the search box. Tap the drug.
  2. The sale form opens with the quantity box ready. Choose the unit you are selling (packet, strip, tablet… the names come from that drug), enter the quantity and check the price.
  3. Tap Add to Cart. Add more drugs the same way. Tap Edit on a cart line to change it; you come back to the same place in the cart.
  4. For a credit sale switch on credit and enter the customer name, phone and the date they promise to pay.
  5. Tap Complete sale. A receipt appears with Share Receipt and Save to phone.

The app takes stock from the batch that expires first. An unfinished sale is kept if you leave the screen, so you can come back to it.

4. Receipts: share, copy and save

Every Share button (receipts, reports, lists, operator logins) opens a small box with two choices:

  • Share to an app — opens your phone's list of apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, email…).
  • Copy text — copies it so you can paste anywhere.

Save to phone puts a text file in your phone's Downloads → MediStock folder. Receipts show the business name, shop name and location, phone number, date, cashier, items and total.

5. Purchases (buying stock)

Open Purchases, search the drug, enter how many packets / strips / tablets arrived, the cost, supplier, batch number and expiry date, then add it to the cart and save. Stock goes up and the batch is recorded. You can buy on credit from a supplier by choosing credit and giving a supplier name and due date.

Tap Edit on a cart line to change it; you return to your place in the cart.

6. Inventory (your drug list)

The Inventory screen lists every drug with its stock, shown the way it is packaged (for example 3 Packets, 2 Strips, 4 Tablets). Tap Low stock only to see what is running out. Tap a drug to edit it; after you save, the list returns to the place where you were.

To add a drug follow How to add a New Drug at the top of this guide.

7. Batches and expiring drugs

Batches lists every delivery with quantity and expiry date; filter Expired or Expiring ≤30d. Expiring Drugs groups stock by how soon it expires (30, 60, 90, 180 days) so you can sell or return it in time. Both can be shared as a list.

8. Stock Taking (counting the shelf)

Search the drug and tap it. The boxes you fill in use the drug's own units, for example Packets / Strips / Tablets. Type what you counted in each box; the app shows what that means compared with the system. Give a reason (tap a quick reason such as Damaged or Missing), then Save Stock Adjustment. Every difference is recorded permanently.

9. What to Buy

Lists the drugs at or under their reorder level and how much to buy to reach the target stock, so you can make a shopping list.

10. Expenses

Record shop costs (rent, transport, salaries…) with category, amount and note. They reduce profit and cash.

11. Credit / Loans (customers who owe you)

Every credit sale creates or updates a customer account. Open the customer to see what they owe, record a payment, or see the history. Customers past their promised date show as Overdue.

12. Suppliers

Keeps what you owe each supplier from credit purchases and the payments you make.

13. Cash Position

Shows the real cash available and exactly how it is made up: starting cash, cash sales, credit payments collected, purchases, supplier payments, expenses and adjustments. Use Record a Cash Adjustment for owner deposits or withdrawals, other income, or cash found missing or extra. Tap the eye to hide the figure.

14. History, Charts and Reports

Business History lists every sale, purchase and cash event; search or filter by type and period, and share or save any receipt. Charts and Profit & Reports show how the business is doing; the summary can be shared or saved.

15. Shops (owner)

Add more shops under Shops, each with a name, location and code. To work in another shop tap Switch to this. Sell, Inventory, Purchases and the other screens then show that shop straight away. The shop you are working in is written at the top of each screen.

16. Users and operators (owner)

Under Users create an operator for a shop: you give a name and a 6-digit PIN. The app shows the Shop Code, Operator ID and PIN once, so share or write them down. You can deactivate an operator (they are logged out and cannot log in; their past sales stay). A PIN cannot be changed for someone else; give them a new login instead.

Operators only see their own shop. Owners see all shops.

17. Working offline and syncing

Everything you do offline is saved on the phone and uploaded automatically when internet returns. The dashboard shows Changes waiting to sync until they have gone up. Open the app online from time to time, especially before switching phones.

Log out only after the "waiting to sync" note has gone, otherwise unsent changes can be lost.
18. Alerts (notifications)

The app warns you about: low stock (immediately when a sale takes a drug to its reorder level, and a reminder at 8:00 in the morning), expiring drugs, credit due, and changes that have not synced for 24 hours. Tap an alert to open the right screen.

  • Allow notifications when asked, and keep banners switched on for MediStock in the phone settings.
  • On Tecno, Infinix and Itel phones also allow auto-start and set battery to No restrictions for MediStock, or alerts may stop when the app is closed.
  • Test it any time in Backup & Export → Alerts → Send a test alert.
19. Backup and Export

Export full backup saves all your records as a file in Downloads → MediStock. The Excel (CSV) buttons save sales, stock, expenses and customers who owe you as spreadsheets. Make a backup every few days and keep a copy somewhere else (WhatsApp to yourself, Google Drive).

20. Updates and help

Most updates arrive by themselves: open the app online twice. A new app file (APK) is only needed for big changes, and it installs over the old one. For help see Developer & Support.
